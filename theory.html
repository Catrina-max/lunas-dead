THEORY

Perception, value, and what the player can know.

The project uses philosophical and mathematical ideas as game-design instruments rather than as exposition.

01

Donald Hoffman

Hoffman's Interface Theory of Perception is useful to Luna's Dead as a design provocation: the experienced world may be treated as an interface rather than as transparent access to an underlying state.

That creates a useful game-engine distinction: state != rendering.

The same event can therefore produce several experienced worlds without requiring the game to declare one observer omniscient.

02

Óscar Olea

Olea's axiological and information-oriented approaches to criticism suggest a second design problem: significance emerges through context, expectation, deviation, and relation.

The game can teach the player the grammar of a room before asking the player to notice what violates that grammar.

An anomaly is not automatically true. It is a reason to revise the model.

WORKING PRINCIPLEHoffman gives us the interface. Olea gives us the anomaly.
